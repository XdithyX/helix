Scenario
--------
A controlled JVM fault fixture repeatedly allocated 10 MiB byte arrays and retained
them in a local collection. Helix analyzed the resulting JFR recording, G1 GC log,
and HPROF heap dump. Local filesystem paths and generated artifact identifiers have
been removed from this excerpt.

Why did this JVM run out of memory?

Cross-Artifact Correlation
--------------------------
JFR identified repeated large byte[] allocations at
JVMFaultSimulator.triggerHeapOOM:67 and recorded the resulting OutOfMemoryError.
The GC log showed humongous-allocation pressure followed by repeated Full GCs that
reclaimed no meaningful memory. The heap dump confirmed that the allocated byte[]
objects remained reachable and occupied approximately 96% of the captured heap.

Correlated evidence chain:
Large byte[] allocations -> humongous-allocation pressure -> ineffective Full GCs
-> OutOfMemoryError

Investigation
-------------
Status: CONCLUDED

Answer: The JVM ran out of memory due to a memory leak caused by the continuous
allocation and retention of large byte[] objects. These objects, each approximately
10 MB in size, were allocated by JVMFaultSimulator.triggerHeapOOM:67 on the main
thread and were not reclaimed by the garbage collector. Evidence from the heap dump
shows that byte[] objects constitute 95.97% of the heap, and java.lang.Thread (main
thread) is the dominant object retaining 94.07% of the heap. JFR old-object samples
confirm that these large byte[] objects were surviving garbage collection. Repeated
Full GCs were observed leading up to the OutOfMemoryError, but they failed to reclaim
any significant memory, indicating that the retained objects were still reachable
and preventing heap recovery.

Evidence tools used
-------------------
- search_findings
- query_heap: dominators and byte-array retention
- query_jfr: OOM, old-object, and allocation evidence
- query_gc: Full GC reclamation and humongous-allocation events

Validation
----------
This conclusion was checked against the fault-fixture source and the raw JFR, GC,
and HPROF evidence. The main thread is the GC root retaining the local collection;
it is not itself identified as the leaking object.
